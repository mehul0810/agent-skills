# Make the world read as real

Start with an observable target, not the word "cinematic." Pick a real-world reference for the player's usual view and one for the closest important object or action. Record the cues you intend to match: dimensions, camera height and lens, light direction, surface finish, and what the material does when it moves. Use a reference for study, not as a shippable texture unless its rights permit that. Prefer owned or cleared photos, scans, meshes and audio; record source, license, attribution and any distribution limits. If no reference is available, state the assumption. Never claim a visual match you could not inspect.

Choose how to **make the main subject**, not just which API will display it. For a fixed camera, use a cleared photo, an original lit render, or original generated artwork if appropriate. If assets must be procedural, bake a lit plate from geometry and material layers: give the casing thickness, let the contact shadow tighten at its base, and reflect the same light differently in glass, metal and worn paint. Keep moving parts, labels and hit areas separate; check their alignment at both ends of travel. Vector ticks over a convincing face can work; an entire scene made of outlined shapes, stock gradients and uniform noise often reads as a diagram. Inspect the exported frame at play size before accepting that choice.

| If the scene reads as a toy | Change this first; judge it from the play camera |
|---|---|
| The room feels miniature; objects float | Set world units from a familiar dimension. Compare proportions, bevel widths, camera height, perspective and contact shadows at that scale. Align the visual surface with the collider; play the approach, not just the hero angle. |
| Everything looks like shiny colored plastic | Give painted metal, bare metal, rubber and wood different highlight widths and surface response. Put wear where hands touch and water collects, not uniform noise everywhere. Check color-space and map assignments (in [glTF](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html), base-color RGB is sRGB, roughness/metalness and normals are data). Check under a neutral light as well as scene lighting. [PBR inputs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/physically-based-materials-in-unreal-engine) support realism; using them does not establish it. |
| Silhouettes read like stickers or a toon shader | Match profiles and natural proportions before increasing texture size. Remove unmotivated ink outlines, banded shading, candy gloss or exaggerated forms. A bright safety marker or clear HUD can stay bright: match the reference rather than desaturating everything. |
| A still works, but the illusion breaks as the player moves | Use a light that belongs in the scene; check shadow direction, exposure, reflections, occlusion, parallax, texture repetition and transitions between levels of detail during a turn. Don't hide errors behind bloom, fog, blur or a narrow camera the brief did not allow. |
| Motion and sound make solid objects feel weightless | Compare acceleration, stopping distance, collision/contact and settling against the observed action. Match [stride to travel](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine) for characters or wheel travel to rotation for vehicles; don't add this pipeline to a fixed dial. Synchronize contact sound to the event and surface; use [distance and room response](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sound-attenuation-in-unreal-engine) where the listener can move. Preserve responsive input. |

Spend detail where the eye and the player go. The developers of *The Vanishing of Ethan Carter* describe how [capture supplied irregular real-world detail](https://www.theastronauts.com/2014/03/visual-revolution-vanishing-ethan-carter/) but [manually placed and simplified foliage](https://www.theastronauts.com/2014/02/approached-3d-foliage-vanishing-ethan-carter/) made their particular views more coherent. Apply the principle, not their toolchain: a well-lit photographic plate can beat a crude 3D prop for a locked camera; a plate that falls apart when the player walks around it cannot.

Review the real-time build beside the reference at the same framing. Capture a normal play view, the closest interaction, and a short movement sequence on the relevant quality tier. Note the most visible mismatch, fix its cause, and repeat the same view/action. Report **visual match**, **control/loop behavior**, and **frame pacing** separately, tied to build, hardware, resolution and settings. Placeholder geometry, an offline beauty render, a generated concept, and a screenshot can each answer a limited question; none by itself proves a photorealistic, playable game at speed.
