# Reference To Storyboard

Use this when translating supplied videos, screenshots, brand systems, or product evidence into an original video direction.

## Reference Review

Inspect the source. Record useful moments:

- source and timestamp,
- observed composition, typography, pace, transition, sound, narrative and audience effect,
- reusable principle, originality boundary, confidence and access/provenance gaps.

Do not describe a reference you could not view. Ask for the file or report the access gap instead of reconstructing it from a title or thumbnail.

Verify accessible video metadata, sample cuts, then watch complete normal-speed motion/audio. Review narrative and typography/composition/motion separately; a contact sheet alone cannot prove pacing, easing, or sound. Record timecodes, not copied footage.

## Enterprise Translation

Choose the film's job before its look: product/material reveal, graphic metaphor, human story, or software workflow. These can combine, but a brand film need not become a UI tutorial. Compare commissioned examples such as [Apple Pro Apps](https://builders-club.com/projects/apple-unleashed), [OpenAI](https://builders-club.com/3d-motion/openai-the-intelligence-age), and [Claude](https://www.motherlondon.com/work/claude-keep-thinking) without treating a company as one style. Sources reviewed 2026-10-04; distinguish creator descriptions, sampled frames, and actual playback.

Translate principles, not signatures: one clear promise, brand-native imagery, negative space, readable type, deliberate rhythm and sound. Show a consequence, not a succession of feature cards. Never copy a reference's distinctive motif, choreography, soundtrack or claims.

For AI or workflow products, show governed inputs, decisions, approvals, integrations, and outcomes. Do not use vague particles, glowing brains, magic automation, or impossible dashboards as substitutes for product truth.

Each shot advances an evidenced idea. Reject stock-office filler, fake customers, arbitrary zooms, subtitle walls and repeated bass hits without narrative purpose.

## Storyboard Contract

Write a visual script before polishing narration: viewer question, visible action, changed state, evidence, and payoff. Choose voice-led or music-led structure; spoken words and screen text should complement, not duplicate, the action. Every shot records:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Time | Start/end frames, duration, reading hold |
| Purpose | One narrative job |
| Visual | Composition and background |
| Copy | Exact screen text and spoken line, or none |
| Evidence | Product/claim source or `conceptual` |
| Assets | Governed paths and provenance |
| Motion | Object identity, before/after state, path/key poses, easing, entrance/hold/exit |
| Audio | Music/voice/SFX cue and license |
| Transition | Outgoing/incoming anchor, overlap, reason; intentional cut or continuous handoff |
| Acceptance | Observable pass condition |

Keep the arc compact: context, tension, product role, verified proof, outcome, next action. A launch teaser can omit detailed UI; an explainer cannot replace a real workflow with abstraction when the workflow itself is the promise.

## Asset Preflight

- Prefer approved SVG logos and licensed fonts; raster logos need alpha, adequate resolution and light/dark edge checks.
- Treat a white rectangle behind a logo as a defect unless the brand specification explicitly defines that container.
- Do not generate exact logos, UI screenshots, wordmarks, or readable product copy. Generate supporting scenes/textures separately and composite governed assets afterward.
- For generated sequences, approve a representative style frame before motion; lock subject/object identity, palette, lighting, lens/camera language, aspect ratio, and reproducibility inputs. Inspect temporal consistency, geometry, unwanted text/watermarks, and frame-to-frame artifacts before compositing governed assets.
- Capture real UI from a verified build and identify version/environment. If UI is unavailable or unreleased, use clearly conceptual shapes without browser chrome, controls, or interactions that could be mistaken for shipped UI.
- Use sanitized deterministic fixture data for product captures. Hide secrets, personal/customer data, internal URLs, test credentials, and notifications. Record the actor/role, product version, viewport, fixture, and capture date. Obtain consent before using a person's likeness or voice.
- For camera/voice capture, lock controlled exposure, white balance, and frame rate; capture clean dialogue/room tone; plan framing, eyeline/background; obtain talent/voice/location releases.
- Preserve truthful chronology. Trimming pauses is acceptable; rearranging states, masking failures, or compositing a result that the product did not produce must be labeled as simulated or excluded.
- Record palette values, type files/weights, source dimensions, licenses, and transformations. Never stretch, crop, recolor, or add effects to a protected mark without approval.

Generated/altered media needs a receipt: tool/source, oversight, rights/consent, changes, realism and disclosure decision. Preserve supported Content Credentials/C2PA; provenance cannot prove product claims.

## Approval Gate

For delegated direction, compare compositions against audience, brand, truth and narrative; validate a representative sequence before scaling. Ask only for requested selection or material brand/claim/scope decisions; exact direction needs no reselection. Internal review is not publication permission.

Enterprise need not mean minimalism. Consider editorial, cinematic, technical or expressive directions; use purposeful imagery or real UI rather than default text/cards. Generate supporting assets, never evidence. Separate exact composition/crop/type/timing fidelity from subjective craft.

## Motion Direction Contract

Do not promise first-render perfection. For new direction, prove a 5-10 second sequence combining typography, product evidence, transition and sound. Reuse approved direction without ceremonial approval; resolve missing assets/claims, never invent UI.

Motion sheet: alignment, safe areas, type, palette, focal hierarchy, camera/depth, timed poses and audio. Define what visibly changes even with the words hidden. An entrance plus a static card is not automatically motion-led storytelling; deliberate stillness for reading remains valid.

- Preserve spatial continuity: retain object IDs, focal anchor, scale and direction through a handoff. Specify start/end poses and overlap; match position and velocity where motion continues, or justify the cut. Avoid arbitrary zooms and unreadable UI perspective.
- Use intentional acceleration/deceleration and settling; reserve linear movement for constant-speed intent. Overshoot, springs, blur and parallax are optional. Inspect in-between frames for path collisions, abrupt speed changes and text warping; frame rate alone cannot establish smoothness. Keep reading holds stable.
- Allocate entrance, readable hold and exit separately. Check exact copy at normal speed on the smallest intended display; no universal word-per-second value replaces this test. Do not synchronize every word or cut mechanically to a beat.
- Map sound cues to visible events, leave breathing room, and keep voice, music and effects separately adjustable. A cue supports meaning, not a weak transition.

Parameterize opening, reveal, UI-action, comparison, workflow, evidence and CTA scenes by assets/copy/timing/aspect, not a borrowed signature.

Frames prove composition, not pacing/easing/audio. Record timecoded defects after playback.
