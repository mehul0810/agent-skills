# Product Video Producer

Turn product truth, brand assets, footage, and visual references into restrained enterprise video with an auditable edit, deterministic outputs, and a bounded learning loop.

## Route

- Reference review, brand translation, storyboard, typography, pacing, composition, motion, or abstract storytelling: read `references/reference-to-storyboard.md`.
- Editing, real software capture, ingest/conform, sound, color, captions, rendering, manifest, QA, or user-critique iteration: read `references/render-review-iteration.md`.
- YouTube long-form, Shorts, thumbnails/titles, channel packaging, disclosure, upload profiles, or retention learning: read `references/channel-delivery-feedback.md`.
- Load only the primary reference for the current stage. If the stage changes, use a compact decision handoff instead of loading the prior and next references together; never load all three references in one working context. A decision spanning two domains may load one supporting reference. Live-verify drift-prone platform rules at delivery instead of carrying every current limit in context.
- Use Creative Production or `imagegen` only for supporting visual exploration/assets. Preserve exact logos, product UI, typography, and copy from governed source files; composite them deterministically rather than asking generation to reproduce them.
- Product claims/copy may route to `content-writer`; WordPress surfaces route to the relevant WP specialist. This skill owns the video artifact and proof.

## Workflow

1. Classify the work: enterprise promo/launch, explainer or software demo, YouTube long-form, Short/cutdown, edit/revision, or delivery-only. Confirm audience, one content promise, channel, duration, conversion or learning goal, deliverables, and review owner.
2. For new production, establish the exact project, source revision or project-file hash, sequence/composition, render job, and expected output role/path. For edit/review/delivery-only work, bind the supplied project or artifact to its existing contract before validation; report missing identity rather than inventing it.
3. Confirm approved claims, brand kit, references, product evidence, sanitized capture environment, audio/likeness/music rights, accessibility needs, synthetic-media decision, and platform constraints.
4. Inspect actual references and footage. Record timestamped observations and distinguish observed cues, inferred intent, reusable principle, and elements that must not be copied.
5. Preflight governed assets, footage, fonts, licenses, UI captures, and generated media. New narrative work needs a brief, beat sheet, and shot-level storyboard with proof and acceptance criteria. Revisions inherit the approved contract; delivery-only work does not create redundant story artifacts. When direction is open, obtain storyboard, animatic, or representative style-sequence approval before the full edit/render.
6. Edit non-destructively from an explicit timeline and render contract. Keep product truth separate from abstract metaphor; never fabricate product UI, customers, metrics, capabilities, or outcomes.
7. Deliver the review render, intentional poster, manifest, accessibility assets, and channel package when relevant. Validate technical metadata, narrative promise, UI truth, typography, logo edges, motion, audio, rights, accessibility, claims, and output identity.
8. For direct critique, apply the exact bounded change and preserve invariants without inventing a hypothesis. For post-publication evidence, record one bounded hypothesis and change only the packaging or edit variable it implicates. Rerender only when the video changed; report remaining proof gaps without silently rewriting the direction.

## Non-Negotiables

- Reference work is analysis, not imitation. Do not reproduce a competitor's sequence, distinctive visual identity, music, copy, or protected assets.
- No white-box logo artifacts: use the approved SVG or alpha asset, validate edges over light and dark frames, and do not redraw a brand mark with generative tools.
- Abstract-product storytelling may explain a verified problem, workflow, control, or outcome, but must not imply unshipped interfaces or unsupported claims.
- Prefer typography, hierarchy, space, timing, editorial rhythm, sound detail, and purposeful transitions over template spectacle. Premium does not mean constant movement, generic AI glow, stock-montage filler, fake customers, or kinetic text that repeats the narration.
- Real UI capture uses an identified build and role, sanitized deterministic fixtures, and consented voice/likeness; it never exposes customer data, secrets, credentials, internal URLs, or notifications.
- A polished edit cannot conceal privacy, consent, rights, accessibility, product-truth, or output-identity gaps.
- Do not publish, release, upload, change live video metadata, start/stop/apply a public experiment, or overwrite an approved master without explicit owner approval.

## Required Handoff

Report the mode and promise, approved direction, edit/storyboard version, project/job/output identity, render profile, output paths, source/asset/rights provenance, claims and disclosure decisions, accessibility/channel package, validation, critique or analytics changes, unresolved gaps, and approval still required.
