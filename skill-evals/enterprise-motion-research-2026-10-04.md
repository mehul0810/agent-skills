## Synthesis

The following are production inferences from the bounded corpus, not universal
rules or measured audience-performance results:

1. Select the communication job before choosing an aesthetic: product/material
   reveal, graphic metaphor, human story, or software workflow. Combine formats
   only when the audience and promise benefit.
2. Build a visual argument. Each shot changes a meaningful state; typography and
   voice support the action rather than substituting for it.
3. Preserve object identity and spatial anchors through continuous transitions.
   Specify paths, poses, overlaps and velocity where relevant; an intentional cut
   is also valid. Higher frame rate alone cannot establish smooth motion.
4. Use readable settled holds and deliberate rhythm. Constant animation, generic
   glow, repeated cards and mechanical beat cuts are not quality criteria.
5. Translate principles, not proprietary signatures. Approved assets, real
   product evidence and a distinct visual direction remain necessary.
6. Inspect risky transitions early, then verify the encoded final artifact.
   Frames support composition and local continuity checks; complete normal-speed
   audiovisual review is a separate gate.

  in-betweens, and keep playback evidence separate from metadata.

  discontinuous handoff diagnosis, and a safe intentional reading hold.

quality, native editor delivery, real-product fidelity, or model equivalence.
